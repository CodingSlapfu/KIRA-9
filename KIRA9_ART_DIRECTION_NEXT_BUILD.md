# KIRA-9 — Owner art-direction acceptance brief (next build)

**Status:** OWNER-REQUESTED / NOT IMPLEMENTED / NOT ACCEPTED  
**Applies to:** Website, browser emulator, 240×240 orange FM-1 firmware face/portrait, animation and consistent branding  
**Baseline feedback:** Current KIRA-9 website character appears unattractive and does not match the agreed late-1980s/1990s Japanese cyberpunk anime direction. This requires a substantive visual identity redesign, not minor cosmetic adjustment.

## Creative target

Create an **original**, iconic and beautiful female cyborg vocalist recalling the craftsmanship and atmosphere of **1990s Japanese cyberpunk anime**, with *Ghost in the Shell* and *Akira* as era/style references. Do **not** copy existing characters, copyrighted character designs, identifiable faces, or movie frames.

She should feel elegant, mature, compelling, expressive, premium, futuristic, and unmistakably anime; not generic, juvenile, western-cartoon-like, uncanny, or a placeholder vector mascot.

## Face / framing

- Strong **close-up portrait** composed for the square 240×240 screen as well as the larger website.
- Refined, attractive anime facial proportions: confident but graceful face, appealing jawline, subtle nose and carefully drawn mouth.
- Expressive, beautifully shaped eyes and coherent lids, pupils, brows and gaze. Avoid blank stares, goofy exaggerated expressions and distorted anatomy.
- Consistent, distinctive face structure and expression model across idle, singing, blinking and emotion states.
- A memorable, stylish cyberpunk haircut and recognizable silhouette, legible at small sizes.

## Cybernetic styling

- Elegantly integrated, credible head/neck/temple/ear augmentations, subtle cranial plates, tasteful panel seams and restrained luminous interface details.
- Details must reinforce the design rather than resemble random glued-on mechanical patches, clutter or visual noise.
- Sleek materials and selective accents; preserve facial beauty, warmth and emotional readability.

## Rendering / palette

- High-end cel-animation / hand-illustrated 90s anime feel: precise linework, coherent cel shading, deliberate highlights, cinematic depth, mature visual design.
- Controlled cool tones, rich shadows and selective neon/tech accents. Avoid muddy colors, overly flat placeholders, messy textures, excessive glow or cheap effects.
- Anime character artwork—not chibi, low-effort vector art, generic web-avatar art, or western-cartoon proportions.

## Animated vocalist performance

- Natural, polished blinks, eye tracking/focus, subtle brows/lids, micro-expressions and restrained cybernetic twitches.
- Attractive and acoustically appropriate vowel/consonant mouth shapes; accurate lip/viseme timing, consonant closure, diphthongs, humming closure and phrase endings.
- Expressive singing without distorted, uncanny or goofy mouth deformation; any servo-like glitches stay rare and **visual only**.
- Smooth motion and convincing timing; avoid distracting loops, jitter or UI/audio lag.
- Reuse or improve the current real expression/viseme signals rather than replacing functioning animation with static artwork.

## Website + firmware consistency

- Update the website's hero character, identity, typography, panels and visuals so they form one coherent, artist-focused sci-fi world.
- Bring the browser emulator and firmware portrait into the same design language. The small display can use simplified detail **but must retain face proportions, silhouette, colors and expression identity**.
- Build original usable production assets, not just written moodboards. Prefer coherent source/asset exports that can be revised.
- Respect the FM-1's 240×240 resolution, memory and frame-time budgets. Preserve usability, boot/update/install/rollback and BLE/audio behavior.

## Explicit anti-targets

Do not ship an awkward/unattractive face, a generic anime girl, chibi/childish proportions, mismatched web vs firmware characters, pasted-on cybernetic clutter, overly flat placeholder art, lifeless expressions, poorly aligned lips, or an uncanny low-effort approximation.

## Acceptance and verification

1. Show clear before/after owner-review previews of **website close-up**, **240×240 firmware portrait**, and **singing expression/viseme frames**.
2. Verify artwork and animation consistency across web/emulator/firmware assets, without overstating hardware parity.
3. Run targeted visual/resource/performance checks; retain already-passed technical gates and rollback support.
4. Keep **owner visual acceptance OPEN** until the owner reviews the actual updated website and FM-1 display.
5. Do not label visual requirements complete merely because artwork files were generated or the website was deployed.

**Owner's acceptance target:** “Now she actually looks like KIRA-9 — a compelling 90s-style cyberpunk anime vocalist.”

**Scope guard:** Preserve all other KIRA-9 features and existing website/installer functionality. Do not modify AQUORA. This brief is an active requirement for the **next KIRA-9 correction build**, not a claim of completed implementation.
